Song Guide

25 Best Hindi Karaoke Songs for Your Next Party

A mix of eras, moods and difficulty levels — so every voice in the room finds something to sing.

The best karaoke playlist isn't just a list of hits — it's a mix that gives everyone in the room a moment to shine. Here's a curated set spanning romantic ballads, wedding-season dance numbers, and a few nostalgic classics that always bring the whole room in on the chorus.

Romantic ballads (great for confident solo singers)

  • Tum Hi Ho — Arijit Singh, from Aashiqui 2. The single most-requested Hindi karaoke song for a reason: emotional, well-known, and rewarding to nail the high notes on.
  • Kesariya — Arijit Singh, from Brahmastra. A modern favourite with a melody most people already half-know from hearing it everywhere.
  • Raabta — Arijit Singh, from Agent Vinod. Smooth, mid-tempo, forgiving for someone still building confidence.
  • Channa Mereya — Arijit Singh, from Ae Dil Hai Mushkil. A genuine vocal challenge — save this one for someone who wants to be tested.
  • Tera Ban Jaunga — Akhil Sachdeva & Tulsi Kumar, from Kabir Singh. A duet-friendly pick if two people want to sing together.

High-energy dance numbers (best for group singing)

  • Kala Chashma — Amar Arshi, Neha Kakkar & Badshah, from Baar Baar Dekho. Impossible not to move to; everyone joins in on the hook.
  • London Thumakda — Labh Janjua, Sonu Kakkar & Neha Kakkar, from Queen. A wedding-season staple that works for any celebration.
  • Nagada Sang Dhol — Shreya Ghoshal & Osman Mir, from Goliyon Ki Raasleela Ram-Leela. High-energy and instantly recognisable from its opening beat.
  • Gallan Goodiyaan — a full ensemble cast, from Dil Dhadakne Do. Built for a big group chorus moment.
  • Badtameez Dil — Benny Dayal, from Yeh Jawaani Hai Deewani. Fast, fun, and forgiving if you don't hit every note.

Retro classics (crowd-pleasers across generations)

  • Tum Hi Ho Bandhu — Sonu Nigam, Shreya Ghoshal & Kavita Seth, from Cocktail. Warm, nostalgic, easy to sing along to even if you don't know every line.
  • Mere Sapno Ki Rani — Kishore Kumar, from Aradhana. A genuine classic that older and younger guests both recognise.
  • Pal Pal Dil Ke Paas — Kishore Kumar, from Blackmail. Gentle, melodic, a good warm-up song for someone nervous to go first.
  • Chura Liya Hai Tumne — Mohammed Rafi & Asha Bhosle, from Yaadon Ki Baaraat. A beloved duet, perfect for two confident singers.
  • Ye Dosti — Kishore Kumar & Manna Dey, from Sholay. The unofficial anthem for any group of close friends performing together.

For the confident solo performer

  • Agar Tum Saath Ho — Arijit Singh & Alka Yagnik, from Tamasha. Emotionally demanding, best attempted once you're warmed up.
  • Tere Sang Yaara — Atif Aslam, from Rustom. A genuine test of sustain and breath control.
  • Phir Le Aya Dil — Arijit Singh, from Barfi!. Understated, moody, rewards restraint over volume.

Easy wins for first-timers

If someone's never sung karaoke before, start them here — familiar melodies, forgiving vocal ranges, and choruses the whole room already half-knows:

  • Tum Hi Ho — everyone knows at least the chorus.
  • Kala Chashma — energy carries you even if the pitch wobbles.
  • London Thumakda — practically impossible to sing badly at a party.
